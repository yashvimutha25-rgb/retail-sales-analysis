# Retail Sales Analysis – Key Findings & Recommendations

*Based on the synthetic practice dataset (1,460 clean order lines, Jan–Dec 2025, ₹ values). Figures match the Excel `Analysis` sheet and the SQL queries.*

## Headline numbers
- **Total revenue ₹60.1 lakh** from **1,460 orders** and **2,926 units**; average order value **≈ ₹4,116**.

## Findings
1. **Electronics leads.** It brings in **29.8%** of revenue (₹17.9 lakh), followed by Home & Kitchen (21.0%). Clothing is the smallest category at 14.7%.
2. **Revenue is concentrated in a few products.** The top 10 products make up **~58%** of revenue. Laptop alone (only 12 units) gives **8.2%** of revenue.
3. **Groceries = volume, not value.** Groceries account for ~49% of units sold (1,426 of 2,926) but only 17.7% of revenue.
4. **Strong festive-season peak.** Q4 contributes **35%** of annual revenue (₹21.0 lakh); October is the best month (₹7.9 lakh, +63% vs September). Q1 is the weakest quarter (₹11.8 lakh).
5. **West and North drive sales.** West contributes 25.8% and North 24.0%; East is lowest at 14.4%.
6. **Discounts are heaviest where sales are weakest.** Clothing averages a **20.1%** discount and Furniture **15.6%**, versus 1.5% for Groceries. Overall about **₹5.9 lakh (≈9% of gross sales)** was given away in discounts, and Clothing alone accounts for ₹2.2 lakh of it – yet it is the lowest-revenue category.

## Recommendations
1. **Stock up and promote early for Q4** – plan inventory and marketing for September–October, since October revenue is ~1.9x the Jan–Jun monthly average.
2. **Protect the high-ticket winners** (Laptop, Water Purifier, Smartphone, Sofa Set) – keep them in stock and feature them in campaigns; consider bundling accessories.
3. **Review the Clothing discount policy** – test smaller or targeted discounts, since deep discounts are not translating into higher revenue.
4. **Grow East and Central** with regional offers or new stores – they have the most headroom.
5. **Use Groceries as a traffic driver** – low-value, high-frequency items can be paired with higher-margin products to lift basket size.

*Limitation: the dataset has no cost data, so conclusions are about revenue, not profit. Adding unit cost would allow margin analysis.*
